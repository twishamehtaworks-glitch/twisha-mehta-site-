About me

A researcher interested in who governs, and in how we study them.

I am Twisha Mehta, a Ph.D. Scholar at the Indian Institute of Technology Tirupati. My doctoral research examines gender and institutional governance frameworks within urban local bodies (ULBs), with an interdisciplinary orientation towards public policy, political institutions, representation, and governance.

My academic journey began at Kamala Nehru College, University of Delhi, where I completed my B.A. (Hons.) in Political Science, building a foundation in Indian polity, political theory, and foreign policy. I then joined IIT Tirupati for the Master of Public Policy programme. My dissertation on women as policymakers in Vadodara, Gujarat became the seedbed for my doctoral work, and it is where I began to ask not only what women in governance do, but how we as researchers study them.

I believe the qualitative–quantitative divide in political science is neither inevitable nor always productive.

That methodological concern now shapes my wider agenda. I use computational approaches, including Natural Language Processing, to analyse political and governance material such as parliamentary debates, alongside policy research, constituency analysis, and city-level budget analysis.

My work has taken me through legislative research in the Office of a Member of Parliament, human-rights research at the NHRC, political consulting, and survey-based fieldwork. I write opinion and policy commentary for outlets including Telangana Today, Policy Circle, and The Policy Edge. I welcome academic collaborations, conversations on gender and governance, and research that engages seriously with methodological innovation.
