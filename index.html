About

Biography

Welcome to my academic website. I am Twisha Mehta, a Ph.D. Research Scholar at the Department of Humanities and Social Sciences, Indian Institute of Technology Tirupati, where I work at the intersection of governance, gender, and electoral politics. My doctoral research examines the political conditions and institutional structures that shape women's participation in formal governance in India, with a particular focus on how women navigate, enter, and exercise agency within electoral and policymaking spaces.

My academic journey began at Kamala Nehru College, University of Delhi, where I completed my Bachelor of Arts (Hons.) in Political Science, developing a foundational grounding in Indian polity, political theory, and foreign policy. I subsequently joined IIT Tirupati for the Master of Public Policy programme. My MPP dissertation investigated women as policymakers in Vadodara, Gujarat — an empirical study that became the intellectual seedbed for my current doctoral work. It was during this period that I began to seriously engage with questions not only of what women in governance do, but of how we as researchers study them, and whether the methods we use are adequate to the complexity of what we are trying to understand.

"I believe the qualitative–quantitative divide in political science is neither inevitable nor always productive."

This methodological concern has become central to my broader research agenda. I am increasingly interested in how qualitative social science material — interviews, political discourse, archival and ethnographic sources — can be subjected to empirically rigorous analysis using computational techniques, particularly Natural Language Processing. My ongoing work attempts to build bridges between the qualitative and quantitative traditions in the study of gender and governance.

I am open to academic collaborations, conversations on gender, governance and electoral politics, and research that engages seriously with methodological innovation in the social sciences.
