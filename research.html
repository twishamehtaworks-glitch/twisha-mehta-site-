Intellectual Pursuits

My scholarly interests span political institutions, gender, governance, and the methods we use to study them — building bridges between qualitative and computational traditions in the study of Indian politics.

I am increasingly interested in how qualitative social science material — interviews, political discourse, archival and ethnographic sources — can be subjected to empirically rigorous analysis using computational techniques, particularly Natural Language Processing. I believe the qualitative–quantitative divide in political science is neither inevitable nor always productive, and my ongoing work attempts to build bridges between these traditions in the study of gender and governance in India.

Themes

Research Areas

Gender & Electoral Politics

Examining women's entry, agency, and participation within India's formal political institutions.

Women's Political Representation in India

Understanding what meaningful representation looks like beyond numerical presence.

Governance & Public Policy

Institutional structures, local governance, and the policy conditions shaping political outcomes.

Natural Language Processing

Computational analysis of political text, speeches, and legislative discourse at scale.

Computational Social Science

Bridging quantitative rigour with interpretive traditions in the study of politics — treating method as a research question in its own right, not just a tool applied after the fact.
